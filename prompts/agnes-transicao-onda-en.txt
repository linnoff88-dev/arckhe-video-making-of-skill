A full-scale wall of real water rises on the open-water side of the tank and travels laterally across the miniature city, away from the camera. No building is damaged until the water touches it: everything ahead of the water line stays intact and upright. It shears the buildings off their foundations one row at a time in the direction of travel: roofs tear off, facades break inward, the broken pieces stay small and tumble low inside the moving water, always at miniature scale with real weight, friction and collision. A second surge pushes the last blocks off the base. The camera does not move: one continuous handheld wide shot, the crew stay dry on the deck, the air above the tank stays clear.

ONLY the miniature breaks apart. The film set itself is never damaged and never enters the water: cameras, lenses, lights, stands, cranes, monitors, cables, flight cases, blue screens and every piece of production equipment stay untouched, upright and in place on the dry deck, outside the pool. Nothing that floats or tumbles in the water is production equipment — every fragment in the water is a piece of the miniature.

# AVCC Prompt Guide

## Image-to-Video — 9:16 BTS

### Practical Wave Tank Miniature Destruction

A copy-ready English guide for transforming one architectural reference image into a **15-second, ultra-realistic behind-the-scenes practical-effects video**.

---

## 1. Project Overview

**Format:** Vertical 9:16  
**Duration:** 15 seconds  
**Style:** Ultra-realistic behind-the-scenes footage  
**Camera:** One continuous handheld iPhone take  
**Scene:** Practical-effects soundstage with a miniature city inside a wave tank  
**Reference:** `@Image1` controls the miniature architecture  
**Framing:** Wide shot  
**Workflow:** AVCC / AIVIDEOSKOOL

The wave, miniature, tank, safety barrier, crew, and production environment must remain visible throughout the sequence.

The scene must feel like authentic footage recorded during the production of a blockbuster film—not like a polished disaster-movie shot.

---

## 2. Timing Structure

The video is divided into three clear beats:

### 0–4 seconds — Setup and Tension

- Reveal the soundstage.
- Show the crew preparing the practical effect.
- Display the clapperboard.
- Hear the director call for action.
- Activate the industrial pumps.

### 4–10 seconds — Main Impact

- A massive practical wave strikes the miniature.
- Buildings physically break apart.
- Roofs, walls, facades, trees, and scenery are swept away.
- A second surge destroys every remaining structure.

### 10–15 seconds — Aftermath

- Remaining fragments wash across the tank.
- The miniature foundation is stripped bare.
- The scale of the physical set remains visible.
- End with the last recognizable fragment leaving the frame.

---

## 3. Why the Prompt Works

The prompt separates four different responsibilities:

1. **Production context**
2. **Reference-image fidelity**
3. **Camera and safety logic**
4. **Destruction chronology**

This prevents the model from generating a generic flood scene and helps maintain:

- Architectural consistency
- Readable action
- Safe camera placement
- Practical physical destruction
- A clear beginning, impact, and ending

---

## 4. Non-Negotiable Constraints

### Reference Fidelity

`@Image1` controls:

- Architecture
- Building layout
- Facades
- Materials
- Colors
- Proportions
- Overall miniature design

Do not add unrelated architecture, landmarks, monuments, fantasy buildings, or decorative structures.

### Wide BTS Framing

Keep the following visible:

- Entire miniature
- Approaching wave
- Reinforced tank
- Safety barrier
- Crew members
- Production equipment
- Soundstage environment

### Dry Camera Operator

The following must remain completely dry:

- iPhone operator
- iPhone
- Lens
- Observation platform
- Foreground
- Nearby crew position

### Practical Destruction

The destruction must feel physical.

Buildings should:

- Break into large sections
- Collapse from their foundations
- Tumble through the water
- Lose roofs and facades
- Wash away as practical debris

Avoid clean, weightless, fantasy-like, or obviously CGI destruction.

### Total Wipeout

By the final frame:

- No building remains standing.
- The miniature base is completely exposed.
- Only water and scattered fragments remain.

### Safe Set Logic

Water and debris must remain inside:

- The reinforced wave tank
- The protected effects zone
- The designated wet area

Crew members remain behind barriers and on protected walkways.

# 5. Full Copy-Ready Prompt

Create a 15-second vertical 9:16 ultra-realistic behind-the-scenes video, casually recorded on an iPhone by a crew member standing at a safe distance from a blockbuster practical-effects film set.

The video must feel like authentic production footage from a large soundstage, not like a polished disaster-movie shot. The viewer is watching the making of a practical destruction effect.

`@Image1` defines the exact architectural appearance, building designs, facades, materials, colors, proportions, and overall layout of the handcrafted miniature.

Recreate the buildings visible in `@Image1` as a highly detailed physical miniature.

Do not introduce Bukhara landmarks, unrelated architecture, additional monuments, fantasy buildings, decorative structures, or any architecture that is not visible in the reference image.

The miniature stands inside a huge reinforced practical wave tank on a large film soundstage.

The production environment includes blue screens, lighting rigs, cranes, high-capacity water pumps, thick hoses, power cables, production monitors, a protected cARCKHÉ camera inside waterproof housing, and several crew members wearing black “AIVIDEOSKOOL” T-shirts.

The iPhone operator films from a slightly elevated, completely dry observation platform positioned approximately 10 to 15 meters from the wave tank.

Use a moderately wide composition that keeps the entire miniature, the approaching wave, the safety barrier, and part of the production set visible.

A tall transparent safety barrier and a wide dry working area separate the operator from the tank.

The wave travels laterally across the miniature and away from the operator.

The operator, iPhone, lens, observation platform, foreground, and nearby crew position remain completely dry throughout the video.

Use one continuous handheld take with natural smartphone movement, realistic exposure, slight focus breathing, subtle rolling-shutter behavior, and authentic behind-the-scenes production audio.

## 0–4 Seconds

The continuous handheld take begins with a slightly distant wide view of the busy soundstage.

The camera slowly pans across crew members making final adjustments to the miniature buildings, which are modeled precisely after `@Image1`.

The protected cARCKHÉ camera is secured inside waterproof housing near the tank.

An assistant places a clapperboard beside the miniature and quickly retreats along a protected dry walkway.

From off-screen, the director shouts:

“Rolling... Action!”

The practical-effects supervisor pulls a heavy industrial lever.

Multiple high-capacity water pumps activate simultaneously with a deep mechanical roar.

Water begins rising inside the wave system while the entire miniature, tank, safety barrier, and crew remain visible in the wide composition.

## 4–10 Seconds

A massive wall of water rapidly rises across the full width of the reinforced tank.

The wave is significantly taller than the miniature buildings.

The iPhone operator remains stationary behind the distant safety barrier and keeps the complete event inside the wide frame.

The enormous practical wave strikes the miniature with overwhelming physical force.

The first impact instantly tears roofs from the buildings and blasts facades inward.

A fraction of a second later, entire miniature structures break away from their foundations, collapse into large physical sections, and are swept across the tank.

Streets disappear beneath violently churning water.

Roofs, walls, columns, doors, trees, facades, and large pieces of handcrafted scenery tumble through the current with realistic weight, friction, collision, and water resistance.

A powerful second surge follows immediately and sweeps away every remaining structure.

Nothing remains standing.

The complete miniature city is ripped from its base and carried downstream in a dense torrent of water and practical debris.

From off-screen, the director yells:

“Keep rolling!”

## 10–15 Seconds

The violent current continues dragging the final large fragments of the miniature across the tank.

Smaller debris spins through foaming water while the exposed miniature foundation is stripped completely bare.

By the end of the sequence, the entire architectural miniature based on `@Image1` has been demolished and washed away.

Only an empty flooded foundation and scattered fragments remain.

The camera maintains the distant wide composition, revealing the massive scale of the practical destruction, blue screens, lighting rigs, pumps, cables, protected crew areas, and the devastated wave tank.

Crew members remain safely behind reinforced barriers.

Water and debris stay entirely inside the protected effects zone.

End on the empty miniature foundation as the final surge carries the last recognizable building fragment out of frame.

---

## 6. Negative Constraints

Do not generate:

- A polished cARCKHÉtic disaster-film shot
- A drone view
- A close-up that hides the miniature scale
- A camera placed inside the wave tank
- Water hitting the iPhone operator
- Water on the lens
- Crew standing inside the wet zone
- Floating or weightless CGI debris
- Buildings dissolving instead of physically breaking
- Unrelated monuments or architecture
- Fantasy structures
- Surviving buildings at the end
- Cuts, transitions, or multiple camera angles
- A wave moving directly toward the camera
- Debris leaving the reinforced effects zone

---

## 7. Final Quality Checklist

Before generating, confirm:

- `@Image1` controls the architecture.
- The format is vertical 9:16.
- The duration is 15 seconds.
- The video uses one continuous handheld take.
- The shot remains wide.
- The operator stays completely dry.
- The destruction is physical and practical.
- The first impact is visible.
- A second surge completes the destruction.
- No building remains standing.
- The empty miniature base is visible at the end.
- The soundstage and crew remain visible.
- Water and debris stay inside the protected tank.
